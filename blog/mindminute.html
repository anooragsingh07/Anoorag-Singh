MindMinute — One Minute to Mindfulness

April 30, 2024 · 4 min read

A short guided break can fit into a busy day. MindMinute is a lightweight web app for taking a minute to breathe, stretch, or pause.

Students and developers can spend long stretches focused on work and forget to pause. I built MindMinute as a small reminder that a useful break does not have to take much time or need a complicated setup.

A deliberate one-minute pause

The app offers breathing and stretching exercises alongside meditation prompts. Animated timers help make the short activity easy to follow: choose a break, let the timer guide the minute, and then return to the task with a moment of distance from it.

Keeping each activity brief was part of the idea. A one-minute option can fit between study sessions or development tasks without asking someone to set aside a large block of time.

Small and accessible by design

MindMinute uses HTML, CSS, and JavaScript rather than a backend service. The experience is designed to work offline, and CSS animations provide movement without adding a large dependency. That made the project a useful exercise in building a complete interaction with a small set of browser technologies.

The constraints encouraged me to keep the interface focused: show the activity, explain what to do, and make the timer easy to understand. A small tool can still feel considered when its purpose is clear and its controls are straightforward.

What I learned

This project made me think about how a web app can support a simple human need without overbuilding. I practiced responsive layouts, timing interactions, and making an experience that can be used without an account or a network connection.
